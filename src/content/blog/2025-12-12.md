When I was in college I used this thesis as an essay topic for my English class and it sucked. It was a bad paper. It poorly linked ideas, had bad evidence, and was just an awful read. I think it was one of my worst graded papers. So as I begin writing this post, I'm just really hoping I don't once again write something truely awful.

However, this is something I am very passionate about. We humans love rationalism; we love a good story that ties loose threads of logic together into something that helps us make sense of the world. I'm no better, I love it too. I listen to many podcasts and read many books where rational ideas are explored. But that's the thing, rationalist takes on the world are just ideas. They aren't based in evidence and therefore have no foundations when challenged.

If you are scratching your head wondering what I'm talking about, to be rational is to be logical, yes, but logic has limitations. For example, let's say that there's a popular podcaster out there who talks about things outside of his domain of knowledge a lot. Like maybe a shocking amount. I don't know let's call him, Handers Bubermann. Maybe Handers makes the claim that taking caffeine within 2 hours of waking up is actually terrible for your mental clarity because caffeine binds to androgen receptors that you need in order to fully wake up. It is logically correct that androgen receptors help you wake up in the morning. It is logically correct that caffeine binds to them and thus inhibits them from binding to other things. However, in practice, such an observation has never been seen. Or let's talk about the claim that intermittent fasting increases human growth hormone and therefore would cause an increase in hypertrophy. That all makes sense, we know HGH does indeed increase when fasting. We know that HGH leads to more hypertrophy. However, when tested, intermittent fasting has been shown to inhibit hypertrophy, not supplement it.

These ideas peddled by the good Handers Bubermann are logical and a little evidence based, but they don't pan out in the real world. And no one is dumb or even mallicious to peddle or believe logical, rationalist thinking. It isn't stupid to say "if A is true and B is true, A+B must be true". And occasionally, it is correct. There are for example, rationalist theories that most of us accept. The theory of gravity is a major one. We can piece together a lot of observational evidence for gravity and logically we arrive at the theory of gravity. That isn't a stupid take.

However, many people fall for rationalist arguements in spite of evidence against the logical take. It is one thing to build a way of seeing the world built on rationalism with a lack of evidence. It is a whole other thing to build a rationalist view inspite of emperical data. But so many people do so. Humans are hard-wired to like stories and find patterns. Logical and rational takes on the world have an inherent amount of authority in our minds because they instantly make sense to us. And if they make sense, then all other things be damned.

## "Show me the proof"
Rational minded people are lead down specious reasoning all the time by very good logical stories. If something makes sense to us, we want to believe it. The problem is that proof is an extremely difficult burden to clear.

If I told you that skin cancer was on the rise at the same rate as sunscreen sales. It is somewhat logical to conclude that sunscreen causes skin cancer. In fact, not only is it logical, it has empirical data. I can show you those charts. The problem is that what is hidden behind those charts are hundreds of probablities and connections.

Obviously, causation does not mean correlation. In the case of sunscreen causing skin cancer, there are literally hundreds of factors that could also contribute to both increased skin cancer rates and increased sunscreen usage. Hey, maybe they are right, but with each correlation I can build, the probability of such a statement or maybe even just a singular statement being correct drops dramatically.

Causation vs correlation is a simple example of this kind of critique. But there are literally hundreds of critiques you can make when presented with data. Emperical decision making doesn't just mean accepting any data put in front of you, it means critically analyzing data. But that is a very difficult skill to learn. That is why very smart stoke brokers rely on quants. It is a skill to tease data apart. I don't think it is very logical to suggest that most people can nor have the desire learn that skill.

## Results matter
I think most people are capable of teasing apart results. However, we so rarely do. How many times has the boy called wolf on AI replacing web developers in a short timeframe? Me and all my friends and family weren't supposed to have jobs "6 months from now" at least 3 times. We can all go to ChatGPT. We can all type in "give me a fan website for The Simpsons" and get a very good looking HTML document. We all see it. We all know that web developers are overpayed and all they do is post on Reddit how easy their job is and how they get paid to Google stuff. (Side note: fuck those guys. I'm the engineer constantly cleaning up the code of those lazy assholes.) They're expensive and replacable. And now we've built the perfect machine to replace them. So then `A + B must equal A+B`.

But it hasn't happened at all yet. Maybe it will, but it hasn't yet. People right now are claiming, "junior developers aren't getting hired right now" and "layoffs are rising due to AI". However, we also have a lot of other correlations right now. For instance, most companies are currently in a bear market. Removing the Magnificent Seven from any portfolio will show that most stocks are currently trending down. Why would most companies be hiring in this economy? Job postings for outsourced work have risen also during this time. The college graduation rate is one of the lowest in recent history. There are so many things that correlate. Correlation does not equal causation. And yet people construct this rational AI dogma every day.

Now look, I'm no fool, I do think web developer jobs are going away eventually. I think a lot of jobs could be going away with the rise of AI. But I think if that's what you're hedging your bet on with AI, I just don't see the probability of it paying off. Companies like Devin and Cursor have made a mint selling the story of a low-to-no employee future. Many AI companies in many sectors are selling the same story. And yet, it seems like the adoption rate of these tools are floundering or are unsuccessful.

I personally believe that AI has a bright future, but the money being thrown at the belief in a tech oligarchy run on LLM agents is misplaced. It is a pretty rational take on what people see in the world, but I think outcomes are quite different than how the story goes.

## LLM's are the perfected rationalist mind
Now, if you couldn't see where I was going with this, that's on you. That's right, it's another "Coding Akita AI Hitpiece" (copywrite 2025). The current state of AI is rationalism distilled. In fact, it is literally how it works.

For those unaware (very few of you at this point I feel like), LLM's like ChatGPT, like Claude, and like Gemini are built on a couple of layers of vectorized tokens that only has one goal, "What is the next most likely token in the sequence of tokens". "If I have token A, and I have token B, then token C must follow." Its intelligence most likely stems from the fact that the most likely outcomes are built on natural language which is often very rational.

But that's the thing, to construct a rationalist thought is not intelligence.

If I were to tell you that earthquakes happen because your mom is so fat that when she walks the earth trembles, you probably wouldn't believe me. Internally, you can deconstruct my argument very quickly. Maybe your mom is fat, so fine that line of argument is fine. But you've been around her many times and not felt the Earth beneath your feet move. You're pretty smart, so you know I'm full of shit.

You might laugh, but in experimenting with LLM's they are willing to accept this idea and hullicinate evidence the second I make it sound even slightly rational. "Crazy news in the world of science today, Professor Fuji Yokohama at the Nippon National Institute of Geology published a wild paper. It found that the old theory about tectonic plate shift was wrong. It turns out that the far more likely reason for earthquakes is that incredible amounts of mass concentrated into small areas cause tremors that build in a wave form across the Earth. Most earthquakes can actually be explained by your mom walking around." Instantly believed. The LLM even provided me with a website where it was published. The website of course, did not exist.

That is perfected rationalism. A slight injection of nonsense completely colored the conversation because the LLM just says the next most likely thing. Which if there was a study, people online tend to just accept anyways without fact checking anything about a study. (I know people won't believe this, but you as a regular person, are allowed to criticize published scientific papers. You also are allowed to be bumfucking wrong. But you are allowed to do it.)

This is a major problem with all LLM's and a major problem with the way people interact with them. If I want the LLM to confirm my worldview with logic, it will bend over backwards to do it because that is all it knows how to do. 

## The great hype divide
I think we can all acknowledge at this point that there are people who love LLM's and those who don't and those who don't really care. If you want to know my stance, I'm team "it's really nice there's something that can take arbitrary input and create organized output but my god is it dumb". I find calling LLM's "Artificial Intelligence" to be a misnomer. There is very little intelligence about them. Having infinite knowledge and the ability to produce language does not make you intelligent. It makes you talking documentation.

But I digress, because right now, I guarentee there is someone who is bright red or just mildly chaffed with my assessment of AI. Maybe it really has changed the way they work. Maybe they have built a business that they are supporting a family on with AI. So having some wild-eyed engineer bash the thing that has legitimately changed their life is a little annoying.

Look, I can understand all of that. However, people have to understand that the divide between people who hate AI because we use it as everyday tooling vs. people who use AI because it is a well of infinite knowledge that you can converse with to better understand the world is a huge gap.

I remain a complete skeptical that AI can replace even web developers. And trust me, I don't really care what happens to web developers because I would love to escape this racket. AI is so phenomenally dumb that I cannot fathom it taking over web development. And I'm skeptical because I have seen the output of AI.



## The Rational LLM Hypetrain
If I open Cursor right now, and I type in, "build me pong playable on a mobile phone" I am almost certain that it can accomplish this task. I have had AI make me many such things. I can ask it for a web page that chronicals the Roman empire from rise to fall in a stylistic fashion. I can now even go to Google right now, pay some money, and make a YouTube video that has a realistic human discussing the implications of the latest Federal Reserve press conference and have it mostly be correct.

All of this sounds pretty sweet, LLM's are a world changing technology. To see it any other way is an insane take. It's a paradigm shift. The two questions are though, what kind of paradigm shift is it and what is the scale?

Cooperations are betting that the paradigm shift moves in their favor. And it's a good bet to make, for years out-sized tech monopolies have been able to stifle all competition with huge amounts of resources built by being government sanctioned monopolies. And it also logically follows that high-priced white collar workers will be replaced by LLM's because LLM's can do most jobs.
