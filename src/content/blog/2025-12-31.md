According to many AI CEO's, my job as a software engineer should've been replaced in 6 months about 3 times already. However, engineering still exists and is going fine. Sure it isn't booming like it was in 2020, but the software market is still actually slightly better than many professions. Especially if you have even moderate amounts of experience.

You would not know this though from the way the internet talks about the current state of software engineers. Even software engineers themselves contribute to the gloom by mentioning that AI already exceeds their skillset and they don't see a path forward. (We'll forget about how many of these commenters pre-AI were telling people about how software engineering was "just googling things" and how they got paid "six figure salaries for 10 hours of work", but I digress.) Pretty much everyone believes that software engineering is a dying profession. And I believe it is, but there's a lot of nuance that people are forgetting about.

## Will AI Replace Engineers?
Yes.
It will also replace: accountants, lawyers, doctors, political scientists, pollsters, reporters, artists, movie-makers, marketers, medical labratory scientists, and etc. There is literally no job in the world that is actually safe from automation and AI. LLM's are perfect for replacing white collar workers, but robots and other AI platforms are improving every year. Everyone's job is at risk of AI replacing them. If you think your job is safe, you are clown. It may not happen in 5-10 years, but in 100 years your job will be likely not exist. That isn't a huge surprise, most jobs from 1925 also don't exist anymore. People find new things to do as technology and culture shifts. Web developers didn't exist until really like the 90's.

For the record, I think software engineers are in a unique position of being replaced quicker than others simply because most people who work in AI are themselves software engineers. So coding has become a major feature of general LLM models. There's more momentum there and there's more innovation happening in that space. I would bet that software goes away before something like medical coding goes away. Most software engineers aren't really aware of something like medical coding, so they largely ignore innovating in that space.

However, I also don't think that engineers are going to be replaced any time soon.

## AI Still Sucks At Being Part of a Human Organization
Regardless of the story surrounding AI, it still is not being adopted in the commercial space at a meaningful rate. There has been 3 years for AI to show why it is useful and I would say that it has ultimately failed. Manangers are practically begging subordinates to come up with ways AI can be used in the organization and most organizations are unable to figure out how to deeply in-grain AI into business processes.

The problem is AI still requires huge amounts of other processes to work as it should.
